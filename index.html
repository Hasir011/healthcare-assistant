Health Tips

Small, practical ideas for food, movement, sleep, and stress.

🥗 Healthy Eating

  • Balanced diet importance: A balanced diet provides essential nutrients for your body to function correctly.
  • Fruits and vegetables: Aim for at least 5 portions of a variety of fruit and veg every day.
  • Protein sources: Include beans, pulses, fish, eggs, and lean meat.
  • Limiting processed foods: Reduce intake of foods high in salt, sugar, and saturated fats.

💧 Hydration

  • Importance of water: Crucial for digestion, absorbing nutrients, and regulating body temperature.
  • Regular hydration tips: Carry a water bottle and drink regularly throughout the day.
  • Signs of dehydration: Dark yellow urine, feeling thirsty, dizziness, and dry mouth.

🏃 Physical Activity

  • Benefits of exercise: Improves heart health, muscle strength, and mental wellbeing.
  • Staying active daily: Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate intensity activity a week.
  • Starting small: Begin with 10-minute walks and gradually increase the duration.

😴 Sleep

  • Adequate sleep importance: Vital for physical health and brain function.
  • Consistent schedule: Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day.
  • Sleep hygiene tips: Keep your room dark, quiet, and cool. Avoid screens before bed.

🧘 Stress Management

  • Relaxation techniques: Try deep breathing, meditation, or yoga.
  • Taking breaks: Step away from your work or stressful situations regularly.
  • Healthy routines: Maintain a balance between work, rest, and play.

Common Health Conditions

Clear educational overviews with reminders to seek professional advice.

Diabetes

  • What it is: A condition that causes a person's blood sugar level to become too high.
  • Signs: Feeling very thirsty, peeing more frequently, feeling very tired.
  • Risk factors: Family history, age, being overweight.
  • Lifestyle: Healthy eating, regular exercise, weight management.
  • When to seek help: If you experience symptoms, see a doctor for a blood test.

High Blood Pressure

  • What it is: When the pressure of blood in your blood vessels is unusually high.
  • Signs: Often has no symptoms, which is why it's called a "silent killer".
  • Risk factors: Age, family history, high salt intake, lack of exercise.
  • Lifestyle: Reduce salt, exercise regularly, limit alcohol, quit smoking.
  • When to seek help: Have your blood pressure checked regularly by a professional.

Obesity

  • What it is: Having a high amount of extra body fat.
  • Signs: Having a Body Mass Index (BMI) of 30 or higher.
  • Risk factors: Poor diet, lack of physical activity, genetics.
  • Lifestyle: Calorie-controlled diet and regular physical activity.
  • When to seek help: Consult a doctor or dietitian for a safe weight loss plan.

Vitamin Deficiencies

  • What it is: Not having enough of certain vitamins in your body.
  • Signs: Fatigue, shortness of breath, dizziness, pale skin (varies by vitamin).
  • Risk factors: Poor diet, certain medical conditions affecting absorption.
  • Lifestyle: Eat a diverse, nutrient-rich diet.
  • When to seek help: If you feel consistently unwell, a blood test can identify deficiencies.

Common Lifestyle-Related Problems

  • What it is: Conditions arising from daily habits (e.g., back pain, digital eye strain).
  • Signs: Aches, pains, dry eyes, headaches.
  • Risk factors: Sedentary behavior, prolonged screen time, poor posture.
  • Lifestyle: Take regular breaks, practice good ergonomics, stay active.
  • When to seek help: If pain is severe or persistent, see a physiotherapist or doctor.
This information is for general awareness only. It does not constitute medical advice. Please consult a qualified healthcare professional for diagnosis and treatment.

Emergency Information

India emergency numbers and when to contact trained services.

⚠️ For serious or life-threatening situations, contact professional emergency services immediately.

📞 Emergency Numbers (India)

  • National Emergency: 112
  • Police: 100
  • Fire: 101
  • Ambulance: 102 / 108
  • Women Helpline: 1091
  • Child Helpline: 1098
  • Mental Health (iCALL): 9152987821

🚑 Basic First Aid

Cuts & Wounds

Wash hands, stop bleeding with pressure, clean wound with water, apply dressing.

Minor Burns

Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 mins. Do not use ice or creams immediately. Cover with cling film.

Fainting

Lay the person flat, elevate legs if possible. Loosen tight clothing. Ensure fresh air.

Choking

Encourage coughing. Give up to 5 back blows between shoulder blades. Give up to 5 abdominal thrusts.

🚨 Emergency Response

  1. Assess: Check the area for danger.
  2. Make Safe: Ensure safety for yourself and the victim.
  3. Assess Victim: Check for response and normal breathing.
  4. Get Help: Call emergency services immediately if needed.
  5. Provide Care: Perform necessary first aid until help arrives.
This page provides general awareness information only. For actual medical emergencies, always contact qualified emergency services.
